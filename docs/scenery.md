# Scenery and the room

Railplot 0.16 draws the plan the way the layout will look: plywood boards, ballast, sleepers and rails. On top of the track you can place buildings, trees and roads, and put the whole layout in its room. Scenery and the room show in the 3D view and in the drive view.

Everything here is optional. A plan with no scenery opens and saves exactly as before.

## The plan

The plan starts in the realistic style. **View > Realistic track and boards** switches to the plain diagram (thin rails on pale sleepers), which is quicker to read on very large layouts. Heights mode always uses the diagram so the gradient colours stand out.

Points are drawn with their long timbers, the tiebar and the crossing. Exports to PNG use the style on screen; SVG templates stay as plain true-scale drawings.

## Outlines for planning scenery by hand

**File > Export baseboard outlines for scenery (PDF)…** saves a black-and-white drawing of the baseboards to print and draw on with pen and paper. (The scenery planning sheet next to it in the menu is a picture of the plan with a grid; this one is a line drawing at an exact scale.)

- **Paper**: A4, A3 or US Letter. Railplot turns the page whichever way fits better.
- **Scale**: *Fit on one page* picks the largest round scale that fits (1:10, 1:12.5 and so on), or choose a scale and the drawing is tiled over as many sheets as it needs. Each sheet says which row and column it is; trim at the frame and tape them edge to edge. 1:1 gives full-size templates.
- **Track**: light grey to sketch over (the edges of the sleepers and a dashed centre line), black, or left out.
- **Grid**, **baseboard names** and the outlines of **scenery already on the plan** can each be switched on or off. If the plan has a room, its walls are drawn as thin lines.

Every sheet has the scale and a scale bar. Print at 100% ("actual size"), not "fit to page", or the scale will be off.

## Scenery

Press **Scenery** on the toolbar (or choose *Scenery and buildings* in the list on the left).

| Group | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Stations | Platform, station building, shelter, signal box, footbridge, platform lamp |
| Buildings | Terraced houses, goods shed, one-road engine shed, factory, water tower, lineside hut |
| Signals and lineside | Colour light and semaphore signals, buffer stop, level crossing, fence, telegraph pole |
| Bridges and tunnels | Tunnel mouth, road bridge over the line, plate girder bridge, retaining wall |
| Trees and ground | Broadleaf tree, fir, bush, hedge, grass, ballast or gravel, pond, hill |
| Roads and people | Road, car, lorry, people |
| In the room | Bed, cupboard or shelf unit, backscene board |

Pick an item and it appears in the middle of the plan, selected. Drag it into place; <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd> turns it 15° at a time. In **Details** you can type its size (length, width or height, where the item has them), its rotation, its height above the board and its colour.

Buffer stops jump onto the nearest open track end. Engine sheds, tunnel mouths, level crossings, road bridges and girder bridges turn to follow the track they are dropped on.

Sizes are for OO (1:76). On an H0 plan everything is drawn at 1:87. Furniture and backscenes are full size.

## The room

**Tools > Room…** adds the room: width, depth, where the left and front walls are on the plan, ceiling height, and how high the baseboard tops are above the floor. Tick the sides that have no wall (a doorway, a loft railing): a railing is drawn there.

The room shows as an outline on the plan and as walls and a floor in the 3D view. Beds and cupboards stand on the floor, under or beside the boards.

## In 3D

Press <kbd>3</kbd>. Scenery stands on the boards with the track; with a room, the floor and walls are drawn, the boards stand at their real height and get legs. **Room** and **Legs** in the bar at the top switch those off (turn Legs off for boards that hang on wall brackets). Walls are see-through from outside, so you can always look in.

Every model is generated by Railplot itself; nothing is downloaded. For trains, see the drive view (<kbd>D</kbd>), which has its own rolling stock.

## In the file

Scenery is saved as `objects`, the room as `room` (see [The .railplot file](file-format.md)). Older versions of Railplot ignore both and keep them when saving.

Claude and ChatGPT can see scenery and the room through `get_layout`; proposals still cover track and baseboards only.
